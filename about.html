Our story

The yard behind the plant

In 2009 our founder was a shipping manager watching inbound coil and castings pile up in an unhardened yard. Trucks arrived, drivers waited, nobody knew where a container was, and the plant paid for it in idle production time.

He bought four used tractors and leased the yard behind the plant. The proposition was narrow and unglamorous: know exactly where every piece of freight is, at all times, and tell the customer before they have to ask.

That is still the product. Everything we have added since — ocean, air, bonded warehousing, in-house customs, project cargo — exists because a customer asked for it, not because a market report said it would grow.

How we operate

Four rules we do not trade away

Written down in 2009, reviewed every January, and the reason clients stay for an average of 7.4 years.

01

One owner

Every shipment has a named operator who owns the file end to end. Not a queue, not a shared inbox, not a sub-contracted leg.

02

Tell them first

If a shipment is going to miss its window, the customer hears it from us with a revised plan, before they discover it themselves.

03

Own the assets

Fleet, racking, forklifts and broker licences are ours. When a lane breaks, we fix it rather than forward the call to someone else.

04

Price it honestly

Line-item accessorials, published fuel surcharge, no surprise demurrage. If a cheaper route is worse, we say so in writing.

Safety & compliance

A 0.42% incident rate and the work behind it

Freight has a bad safety reputation. We treat that as a design problem rather than a culture problem: engineered controls first, procedure second, exhortation last.

  • Engineered first. Dock locking, trailer interlocks, blind-spot cameras and load-securement standards fitted to spec, not to budget.
  • Near-miss reporting. Every near-miss is logged, root-caused and closed. The count is rising, which is the point — it means people are reporting.
  • Hours compliance. ELD-enforced driving hours across the fleet, audited quarterly by an independent third party.
  • Certified handling. Dangerous goods, DG air handling, rigging and heavy-lift certifications held in-house and kept current.
